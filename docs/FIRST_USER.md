# Landing your first user

The challenge gives bonus points for a first user. A real conversation beats a polished feature.

## Who

Freelance pentesters, bug-bounty hunters who write reports, VAPT consultants at small firms, security-club mentors who review student reports. Reach them where they are: LinkedIn DMs, Discord servers for CTF teams and security communities you already belong to, X/Twitter replies to people who post about report writing.

## DM templates (edit, keep it short)

**Cold, specific**
> Hi <name>, I am a CS student building an offline tool that turns a tester's raw notes and screenshots into a draft finding (Gemma runs locally, nothing leaves the laptop). Could I run ONE sanitized finding of yours through it in a 15-minute call and ask what is missing? No real client data, a lab or past CTF finding is fine.

**Community post**
> Building a local-only pentest report helper for DEV's Hacktoberfest challenge. Looking for 2 people who write VAPT or bug bounty reports to try it on a sanitized finding and tell me what is wrong with it. 15 minutes, I will share the repo.

**Mentor/club**
> Could your students' last lab report be my test case? I will show you what the tool flags (missing repro steps, unsupported claims, leaked secrets). Free, local, open source.

## The 15-minute session

1. (3 min) Ask how they write findings today and how long one takes. Write the number down.
2. (7 min) They bring one sanitized finding. You run it. They read the draft out loud. Do not defend the tool.
3. (5 min) Ask: What would you not trust? What is missing? Would you use it on a client engagement? What would you pay per month, if anything? What would you want next?

## Ethics and honesty

- Sanitized or lab material only. Never accept a real client report. Never commit anything from a session to the repo.
- Ask before quoting or naming anyone.
- Report n honestly. "2 testers tried it" is fine. Do not turn a compliment into a customer.
- If nobody pays, write that. "Would pay" and "paid" are different sentences.
